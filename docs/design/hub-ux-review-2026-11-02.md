# Hub UX review: what an Apple reviewer would reject

*Evidence note: the gallery in `/tmp/console-react-shot/hub-recon/` was built at 12:44 UTC from the
pre-hub page; `Rail.tsx`, `App.tsx`, `HomePage.tsx`, `DevicePageShell.tsx` and `DeviceCard.tsx` were
rewritten after 13:50 and are uncommitted, and `app.css` has not been updated for them yet (12:43).
So the screenshots judge the craft the new shell inherits (`app.css`, `tokens.css`, `AuditTable`,
`TerminalWorkspace`, `Modal`) and the current fleet screen; the new shell is judged from its source,
and Home/Capabilities/Try it/Plugins/Connect from their spec. No pixels of the new shell exist —
that is finding M12.*

## Verdict

The direction is right: five destinations, the fleet as Home's zero state, Connect as a verb, no
status strip. Deletion is the strongest part of the spec, and the map from today is honest about
what dies. What would fail the review is not the IA but the finish: on the fleet the eye lands on
three identical orange buttons, on Activity on a row of filter chips, on Files on a nine-line
paragraph — and the new Home, Capabilities and Plugins specs keep that habit inside a calmer frame.
The shell is being rewritten as this is written, so the urgent work is to hold the line on the craft
findings below and to define Try it's states before anyone calls the sheet done. Nothing here argues
against the five destinations; two findings (M1, M3) are already visible in the new, uncommitted
shell.

## What the spec gets right

- **Five destinations, fleet as Home's zero state, Connect as a verb, no status strip.** This is the
  decision that makes the console the hub's window instead of a device control panel. Do not reopen it.
- **The live catalogue is the page's read**, with the static registry as fallback, and the page never
  offers a tool the device would refuse. That is the difference between a catalogue and a screenshot.
- **A plugin is an MCP server, installed from a directory, with no sandbox, signature or marketplace**
  — and its tools are ordinary tools behind the same token and in the same record. The trust
  sentence sits at the install; the token remains the only brake. No second policy model.
- **Destructive follows MCP's defaults**, and destructive actions are never the accent fill. This is
  the right amount of policy for v1.
- **One `Sheet`** with a focus trap, Escape, focus return, and a complete product under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Try it as both the demo and the debugger**, plus the confirmation for destructive calls and the
  result shown verbatim.
- **Degradation is designed, not discovered**: no Add button on an old build, no fake data offline.
- **Deletion with names** (Memory/Monitors/Events/Security, the duplicate connect dialogs, the
  per-page pickers) and acceptance lines a reviewer can check. The copy deck's rules — verbs first,
  no "simply", no policy language — are the reason most of the current strings already read well.
