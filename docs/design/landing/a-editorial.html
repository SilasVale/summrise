What the AI gets

A real machine, not a sandbox with a screen.

Real sessions

PTY, SSH and serial lines with attach, resize and scrollback. The same shell a person would type into.

A browser it can drive

Headless Chrome by default, on the machine's own network and sessions. Visible only when someone asks to watch.

Files, memory, monitors

Read and write within a declared root, keep device-local facts, and watch a long job without holding a session open.

Consent by construction

Every call is a signed grant and an audit row. Destructive tools are refused unless the token says otherwise.

MCP-native

One streamable-HTTP endpoint per device, or /mcp/all for a fleet. Any client, no bespoke SDK.

Watch, and take over

See the live session in the console. Hold the keyboard when it matters, then hand it back — the AI waits.

Two surfaces, one product

The console sees the fleet. The panel sees the machine.

console.saisi.online

From anywhere

Devices dial out to the console — no inbound port, no per-device tunnel. Sign in through Access or an operator token, and every device is one row away.

  • Fleet — who is online, what they run, what to do about the one that is not
  • Sessions — terminal, browser, files, runs, audit, approvals
  • Grants — signed, scoped, revoked in one click
127.0.0.1:18090

On the machine

The panel is loopback-only and has no login: if you can read it, you are on the box. It keeps working when the console does not — which is the only way to answer why the console cannot.

  • Live state — sessions, CPU, monitors, channel health
  • Enrol — paste the code, replace a lost enrolment
  • Repair — start, stop, update, read the last failure
